I built **15 distinct breakfasts**, rather than expanding to 25. Fifteen lets you test cold bowls, two shakes, warm breakfasts, and savory meals without buying a large amount of food that may go uneaten. The recipes share ingredients, so you can keep most of the pantry stocked and buy fresh produce weekly.

I’ve excluded **egg, beetroot, coconut, banana, fish, beef, and pork** from every recipe. Chicken is the only meat. The amounts below make **one adjustable serving**; they are starting points, not a prescribed diet.

## Two things to address alongside the menu

**Check her iron status now.** Because she previously tested low and never received treatment, I would arrange a primary care or OB-GYN visit and ask whether a **complete blood count and ferritin** are appropriate. Breakfast can help her eat more iron, but it cannot tell you whether her stores are low or why. A clinician can decide whether she needs treatment. Pairing beans, lentils, tofu, and fortified cereal with fruit or vegetables rich in vitamin C helps with iron absorption. [NHLBI, NIH](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/anemia/iron-deficiency-anemia?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Plan for nutrients that breakfasts may miss.** CDC recommends **400 mcg of folic acid daily** for people who could become pregnant, including before pregnancy. When you approach trying to conceive, review a prenatal vitamin with her clinician; check its actual label for iron and iodine. Because she eats neither eggs nor fish, also discuss **choline** and a possible **algal DHA** source. Chicken, soy foods, beans, broccoli, and dairy contribute choline, but an egg-free breakfast rotation does not guarantee enough over a whole day. Flax and chia supply a different omega-3 fat that converts to DHA only to a limited extent. [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/about/index.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## The 15-breakfast trial

Use plain, unsweetened yogurt or milk where possible, then sweeten to *her* taste. “Fortified cereal” means one whose label shows added iron; check its ingredients for egg, beetroot, and coconut.

|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Blueberry–walnut overnight oats | ½ cup rolled oats, ¾ cup plain Greek yogurt, ½–¾ cup blueberries, 1 tbsp chia seeds, 1 tbsp chopped walnuts, water or milk to loosen | Mix oats, yogurt, chia, and liquid the night before; add fruit and walnuts in the morning. A filling, familiar starting point. |
| **2** | Strawberry–pumpkin seed overnight oats | ½ cup rolled oats, ½ cup fortified unsweetened soy milk, ½ cup plain Greek yogurt, ¾ cup strawberries, 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds | Soak oats in milk overnight; add yogurt, berries, and seeds. Tests a brighter, less nutty oat bowl. |
| **3** | Apple–peanut butter yogurt oats | ⅓ cup rolled oats, ¾ cup plain Greek yogurt, 1 chopped apple, 1 tbsp peanut butter, cinnamon | Assemble cold, or soften the oats overnight. Tests a creamy, crunchy breakfast without berries. |
| **4** | Mango–almond chia cup | 2 tbsp chia seeds, ¼ cup rolled oats, ¾ cup fortified unsweetened soy milk, ¾ cup mango, 1 tbsp chopped almonds | Refrigerate chia, oats, and milk overnight; add mango and almonds. Different texture from ordinary oats. |
| **5** | Kiwi and fortified-cereal crunch | ¾ cup plain Greek yogurt, 1 kiwi, ½ cup **iron-fortified** egg-free cereal, 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds | Add cereal just before serving. This is the quickest sweet breakfast and tests whether she enjoys cereal crunch. |
| **6** | Strawberry–spinach yogurt shake | ¾ cup plain Greek yogurt, ½ cup fortified unsweetened soy milk, ¾ cup strawberries, 1 loosely packed cup spinach, 1 tbsp almond butter | Blend. The berries dominate the flavor; yogurt and nut butter make it more substantial than fruit alone. |
| **7** | Cherry–cocoa tofu shake | ¾ cup frozen cherries, ½–¾ cup fortified unsweetened soy milk, about 5 oz silken tofu, 1 tbsp peanut butter, 1 tsp unsweetened cocoa | Blend. Tests a dairy-free, dessert-like shake with soy protein. |
| **8** | Chickpea–avocado tomato toast | 1–2 slices egg-free whole-grain toast, ¾ cup canned chickpeas (rinsed), ¼ avocado, chopped tomato, lemon juice, pepper | Roughly mash chickpeas and avocado; pile on toast with tomato and lemon. Beans plus tomato and lemon make this an iron-conscious savory choice. |
| **9** | Lemon lentil and pepper bowl | ¾ cup cooked lentils, ½ cup cooked quinoa, ½ red bell pepper, 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds, lemon juice, olive oil | Warm the lentils and quinoa; add chopped pepper, seeds, and lemon. A particularly useful trial for a bean-based breakfast. |
| **10** | Tofu, spinach, and pepper scramble | About 5 oz firm tofu, 1 cup spinach, ½ red bell pepper, 1 slice egg-free whole-grain toast, olive oil, turmeric or paprika | Crumble and heat tofu with vegetables; serve with toast. Tests the *role* of a savory scramble without using egg. |
| **11** | Black bean–sweet potato bowl | ¾ cup rinsed canned black beans, 1 small cooked sweet potato, ¼ avocado, 2 tbsp egg-free, beetroot-free salsa, lime juice | Reheat potato and beans; add toppings. Fiber, color, and a different sweet-savory combination. |
| **12** | Chicken–broccoli rice bowl | 3 oz cooked chicken, ½ cup cooked brown rice, 1 cup broccoli, lemon juice, olive oil, pepper | Reheat chicken, rice, and broccoli thoroughly; finish with lemon. Tests whether she actually wants chicken at breakfast. |
| **13** | Lemon chicken and white-bean toast | 3 oz cooked chicken, ½ cup rinsed canned white beans, a handful of spinach, 1–2 slices egg-free whole-grain toast, lemon juice | Warm chicken and beans; serve over toast with spinach and lemon. A second chicken test with a different texture. |
| **14** | Warm pear–walnut buckwheat | About ¾ cup **cooked** buckwheat groats, 1 chopped pear, ½ cup plain Greek yogurt, 1 tbsp walnuts, cinnamon | Reheat precooked buckwheat and pear; add yogurt and walnuts. Tests a warm, gently sweet breakfast. |
| **15** | Savory mushroom–edamame oats | ½ cup rolled oats, ½ cup shelled edamame, ½ cup sliced mushrooms, a handful of spinach, 1 tsp sesame seeds, water and a little salt | Cook oats in water; heat in edamame and vegetables, then add sesame. Tests whether oats work for her as a savory food. |

These are **trial recipes, not calculated nutrition prescriptions**. Product fortification, bread size, yogurt type, and her preferred portions will change the nutrients substantially. The variety is deliberate: legumes and tofu provide iron and folate; yogurt or fortified soy milk contribute calcium; fruit and peppers contribute vitamin C; nuts and seeds add fats and texture. Overall adequacy depends on lunch, dinner, and snacks too. [NHLBI, NIH](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/anemia/iron-deficiency-anemia?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Shopping list for one pass through all 15

Buy shelf-stable and frozen items up front. Buy the fresh items in **two smaller rounds** during the trial.

| Keep stocked | Buy or replenish fresh |
|---|---|
| Rolled oats; buckwheat groats; quinoa; brown rice; egg-free whole-grain bread; one egg-free, coconut-free iron-fortified cereal | Plain Greek yogurt, roughly one 32-oz tub; fortified unsweetened soy milk, one carton |
| One can each of chickpeas, lentils, black beans, and white beans; frozen shelled edamame | Firm tofu and silken tofu; roughly 6 oz **cooked** chicken |
| Chia seeds; pumpkin seeds; walnuts; almonds; peanut or almond butter; sesame seeds | Apples, pears, kiwi, avocado, lemons or limes, tomato, bell pepper, sweet potato, broccoli, mushrooms, spinach |
| Cinnamon, cocoa, turmeric or paprika, olive oil; a suitable salsa | Frozen blueberries, strawberries, mango, and cherries can replace most fresh fruit |

**Prep once or twice a week:** cook a little quinoa, rice, and buckwheat; wash and portion vegetables; cook chicken and freeze individual portions. Rinsed canned beans, frozen fruit, and frozen edamame keep morning assembly short. Mix overnight oats or chia cups the **night before**, rather than making a week’s worth at once.

For chicken, cook to **165°F (74°C)**. Refrigerate cooked food promptly, use refrigerated leftovers within **3–4 days**, and reheat leftovers to **165°F**; freeze portions intended for later. Use pasteurized dairy, wash produce, and follow pregnancy food-safety guidance once relevant. ## How I would pick the eventual seven

After each breakfast, have her rate **taste**, **how she feels afterward**, and **whether she wants it again**, each from 1–5. Add a note such as “less thick,” “smaller serving,” or “good on workout mornings.” For a shake, specifically ask whether it kept her satisfied.

Then choose seven she *wants* to repeat. If the highest scores are all sweet bowls, consider whether one well-liked savory choice would make the week more enjoyable, but don’t force a nutritionally admirable recipe she dislikes. Keep one or two runners-up available as swaps. Her comfort and appetite are part of whether this will last.

Finally, treat the reported egg and beetroot reactions as strict exclusions while she seeks medical guidance on them. Read the **full ingredient list** on bread, cereal, salsa, and any future protein powder, and keep preparation surfaces and utensils clear of egg. Egg is a major labeled allergen in the U.S.; beetroot is not on that list, and coconut no longer has to appear in a “Contains” allergen statement. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/have-food-allergies-read-label?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
